On 6–7 October 2026, three members of the Suśruta Project, Dominik Wujastyk, Jason Birch and Kenneth Zysk, took part in the international conference _Nirāmayatā_ (निरामयता): _Healing and Wellbeing in South Asian Texts and Practices_, held at the [India International Centre](https://iicdelhi.in/overview) (IIC) in New Delhi. We were the guests of the IIC–International Research Division (IRD) and its Executive Director, Dr Sudha Gopalakrishnan, who conceived the conference and invited us to speak.

## The IRD and the SAMHiTA programme

The International Research Division grew out of the IIC-Asia Project, which from 1997 onward held annual conferences on the cultures of Asia, for many years under the guidance of Dr Kapila Vatsyayan. Since 2022 the Division has run [SAMHiTA](https://samhita.iicdelhi.in/) (South Asian Manuscript Histories and Textual Archive), a programme supported by the Ministry of External Affairs to inventory, catalogue and digitise South Asian manuscripts held in libraries and archives outside India. Its knowledge partners include the PANDiT project, the Old Indian Institute at Oxford, SOAS, the Wellcome Collection, the Royal Danish Library and the Bibliothèque nationale de France.

The _Nirāmayatā_ conference was organised by the IRD in collaboration with the Indian Council for Cultural Relations and the Bharat ki Soch Foundation, within the framework of SAMHiTA. Its aim was to look at Indian and wider Asian medical traditions through manuscripts and living practice: how medical knowledge was produced, circulated and transformed, and how it moved between Sanskrit, Tamil, Persianate, Tibetan and Southeast Asian traditions. Over two days, six sessions brought together historians, philologists, practitioners of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Sowa Rigpa, and biomedical scientists.

## Our presentations

**Keynote address.** After the inaugural addresses by Shri K. N. Shrivastava (Director, IIC), Dr Sudha Gopalakrishnan, Shri Anil Rajput (Bharat ki Soch) and Shri Shyam Saran (President, IIC), Dominik Wujastyk gave the keynote address, "Achieving Healthiness and the Absence of Illness in Ayurveda." The title takes up the theme of the conference itself, since _nirāmayatā_ means "freedom from disease."

**Dissection in the _Suśrutasaṃhitā_.** In the first session, "Texts and Concepts on Medical Thought," chaired by Prof. G. G. Gangadharan, Kenneth Zysk spoke on "What Medical Literature Can Reveal: Dissection in the _Suśruta Saṃhitā_, Internal and External Transmission." The _Suśrutasaṃhitā_'s instructions for preparing and examining a human body in the _Śārīrasthāna_ are unique in early Indian literature, and raise questions both about how this knowledge was passed on within the medical tradition and about its relations with knowledge from outside it.

**Suśruta on preserving health.** In the afternoon session, "Mind and Body in Asian Medical Traditions," chaired by Dr S. Irfan Habib, Jason Birch spoke on "From _Svastharakṣaṇīya_ to _Svasthavṛtti_: How Suśruta's Chapter on Preserving a Person's Health Changed." The paper traced how the _Suśrutasaṃhitā_'s teaching on keeping a healthy person healthy was reshaped over the course of the work's transmission.

## Other contributions

Our papers sat alongside a wide range of others. Among them were Dr S. A. S. Sarma on Gāruḍa Tantra traditions of snakebite medicine in Kerala; Dr Mehsaan Barawaz on the Persian _Majmūʿah-i Żiyāʾī_ and the meeting of Unani and Ayurvedic learning; Dr Padma Gurmet on Sowa Rigpa; Sugi Lanus on herbal and ritual healing in Balinese _lontar_ manuscripts; Dr Annamma Spudich on European documents about South Indian medicine in the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries; and Prof. Nupur Dasgupta on regional environmental knowledge in Cakrapāṇidatta's _Dravyaguṇasaṃgraha_ and Mādhavakara's _Paryāyaratnamālā_. A session on traditional and modern medicine, with Prof. Bhushan Patwardhan and Prof. Rama Jayasundar, and a closing round table on future directions for traditional knowledge, chaired by Dr M. Maddur Srinivas of NITI Aayog, brought the perspectives of science and public policy into the discussion.
