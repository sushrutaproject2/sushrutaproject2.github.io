On 12 October 2026, Jason Birch and I visited the Āmer Śāstra Bhaṇḍār in Jaipur, a Digambara Jain manuscript library. Our aim was to see a manuscript of the _Suśrutasaṃhitā_ that had been recorded there in the mid-twentieth century.

## The library

The Āmer Śāstra Bhaṇḍār has been housed in several places in Rajasthan over its history. It was formerly known as the Bhaṭṭāraka Devendrakīrti Bhaṇḍār and was originally kept in the Digambara Jain temple of Neminātha at Amber. It was later moved to the Mahāvīra Jaina Bhavana in Jaipur, and then to Śrī Mahāvīrjī.

Today the collection is hosted by the Apabhraṃśa Sāhitya Akādamī evaṃ Pustakālaya (the Apabhraṃśa Literary Academy and Library), an institution of the Digambar Jain Atishay Kshetra Shri Mahaviraji dedicated to teaching Apabhraṃśa, the language of many Jain literary and religious works. The address is [Block B, Malviya Nagar, Jaipur, Rajasthan 302017](https://maps.app.goo.gl/VnTJ1jh2jxebgD6m8), not far from Jaipur's airport.

## The Suśrutasaṃhitā manuscript

Kāslīvāl's list (p. 154) records a single manuscript of the _Suśrutasaṃhitā_, containing only the Kalpasthāna, in 21 folios:

{% include figure.liquid
   path="assets/img/kaslivala-susrutasamhita-entry.png"
   alt="A faint printed Hindi catalogue entry headed Suśrutasaṃhitā"
   caption="Kāslīvāl's entry for the <em>Suśrutasaṃhitā</em> manuscript."
   class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1"
   zoomable=true
%}

The entry reads:

> भाषा-संस्कृत। पत्र संख्या २१. साइज १०॥×४॥ इञ्च। लिपि संवत् १७०२. केवल कल्पस्थान ही है।

That is: language Sanskrit; 21 folios; size 10½ × 4½ inches; copied in Saṃvat 1702 (1645/46 CE); contains only the Kalpasthāna.

Unfortunately, the manuscript recorded in Kāslīvāl's catalogue is no longer in the collection. Smt. Jain produced a handwritten list of the Āyurvedic titles held in the library, and the _Suśrutasaṃhitā_ was not among them. We were not able to photograph the list. It seems, sadly, that [MS Jaipur Āmer Śāstra Bhaṇḍār Kāslīvāl 154](https://panditproject.org/entity/109390/manuscript) was lost at some time between 1950 and 2026.

We thank Smt. Shakuntala Jain for her welcome and for her help in searching the collection.
